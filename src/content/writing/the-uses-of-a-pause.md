A pause is not the empty interval between two useful things. It can be the place where an idea stops performing and starts becoming legible.

The contemporary web is especially good at making every surface feel occupied. A page is considered generous when it offers another choice; a thought is considered finished when it arrives already summarised. The result is a nervous kind of abundance: information with no room to settle.

## Space as a reading tool

In writing, silence is structural. It asks the reader to do a small amount of work, and that work is part of the encounter. A sentence can hold more when it is not immediately pursued by an explanation of itself.

This is not an argument for obscurity. It is an argument for tempo. A publication can make room without becoming vague; it can be clear without insisting on speed.

## A practice of return

The most durable pages allow return. Their details stay available after the first pass, even when the argument has already been understood. A margin, a caption, a quotation, or an image can become a second entrance.

For a small independent journal, this is useful discipline: keep the path visible, but leave enough unmarked ground for the reader to arrive at their own pace.
