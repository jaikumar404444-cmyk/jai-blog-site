To name something is to make it portable. A title lets an image be filed, linked, remembered, and discussed. That is its practical gift. But naming also compresses the moment before a response settles into a category.

## Before the label

Looking is often quicker than we think. We decide whether an image is familiar, useful, beautiful, or irrelevant before the details have fully arrived. The interval between perception and classification is small, but it matters. It is where surprise has a chance to occur.

An editorial page can protect this interval. It can slow the first encounter through scale, sequence, and whitespace without hiding the information that follows.

## Description is not a verdict

Captions are one way to hold both needs together. A good caption adds context and credit. It should not flatten the image into a single approved meaning.

The same is true of an article deck. It gives a reader a reason to enter, then makes room for the text to become more particular than its summary.
