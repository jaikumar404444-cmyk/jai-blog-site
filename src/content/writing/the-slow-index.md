> **Demo content.** This sample exists to exercise the research layout, reference list, and metadata structure.

An index does not simply point outward. It makes a claim about relation: that one thing belongs beside another, that a reader may enter here and travel there.

## The catalogue as an argument

An archive sorted only by date is honest, but not necessarily expressive. Its strength is continuity. A collection organised by subjects, forms, and returning questions introduces another kind of truth: a body of work has patterns, even if those patterns were not visible when each item was first published.

The important distinction is between guidance and constraint. An index should offer paths, not decide where a reader must go.

## Metadata without clutter

For a small publication, a short deck, a type, a topic, and a date do most of the practical work. Additional metadata should arrive only when it helps someone locate, understand, or trust a piece.

This keeps the reading surface quiet while making the underlying collection more durable.

## Notes for a future archive

The archive page in this demo keeps chronology at its centre, then uses type and topic as compact secondary signals. That is enough to scale until real publishing habits reveal a stronger editorial taxonomy.
